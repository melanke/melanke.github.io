## Caption

Your regex isn't slow. It's never coming back. 🔥

`/^(a+)+$/` looks completely harmless. Feed it thirty a's and an exclamation mark, and the engine starts trying every possible way to split those a's between the inner + and the outer +. Each extra character doubles the work.

In Node, the whole process goes down with it. Single thread. And you can't time it out either, because the loop that would fire your timer is the same loop that's stuck.

This is not an exotic edge case. Between January and May this year, ReDoS produced a CVE every 2.7 days.

Last week TC39 moved a proposal called Linear Matching to Stage 1. Four possible designs are on the table, one per card.

A few personal favorites:
→ the flag that refuses to build the regex at all
→ the one where you hand it a fuel budget and hope for the best
→ the reason none of this is the default (last card, and it's the best joke in the set)

Swipe through. Tag the teammate still validating emails with a regex from Stack Overflow 😏

Nothing to install today, it's Stage 1. If your patterns come from users, your move is to leave the engine. node-re2 and regolith both wrap linear ones. Ruby, .NET, Rust and Go sorted this out years ago, the last two by simply deleting the dangerous syntax from their regex flavor.

Which of the four would you actually want in your codebase? 👇
